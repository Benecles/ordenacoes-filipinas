The back, rebuilt

The back of a lesson now draws the lesson as one strip, so you can see how it's built before you read anything.

You sent a screenshot of Contratos Aula 01's back this morning and called it terrible. You were right about why. Most of that page was a list of what the lesson lacked: no source block, no notes, a "no sentence yet" under the figures, a dotted icon for the capabilities it didn't have. Top right sat a faint rectangle that turned out to be the lesson's first figure at seven percent opacity, mirrored, which was meant to look like the front showing through the paper and looked like a smudge.

The data under it was real. What was wrong was that the page answered "what is missing" when your question is what the lesson is, how it's built and whether it's any good. So the back now opens on the title and one line of facts (length against a sitting, chapters, figures, how many of them say what they do, what's quoted), and under that it draws the lesson.

the strip is to scale: 6,000 words is the full width, and the dotted bracket is one sitting

I've been calling it the X-ray. The reader page is parsed in order, and its chapters become hatched lengths on one rail, with the figures marked above it where they sit, the source blocks as strokes below it (in the decided colour when they quote a decision) and the bet and the quiz in their places. Contratos Aula 01 ends at about a third of the bracket and has no strokes under the rail. Processo Aula 04 fills the bracket and has five. You can see the difference between the two courses before reading a word of either, which was the point of the generation dress too.

the "next" line still carries my course notes from the 14th, dated, until I rewrite them

Below the strip are the figures, large, with their data-does sentences or a note that nobody has asked what it does yet. Next to the sources the page quotes, it lists the chapters the pipeline prepared for that lesson, which in Contratos is a little damning: three chapters of Caio Mário and Venosa sit in its compendium and the lesson quotes none of them. My course verdict sits beside it, along with the notes I left on the page if there are any. The life line keeps its ticks and now lists what the commits said. What's missing has shrunk to one line at the bottom, read off the live page.

The other change is that nobody has to rebuild any of this by hand any more. Until today the backstage only changed when I ran its build on this Mac, so it was always as old as my last session. Now the site's publish step checks out the workshop with the deploy key you set up and runs the whole backstage build whenever something merges, figure check included, on a copy of the site. A build that fails leaves the last good backstage in place and puts a warning on the run, so the reader site always goes out. The first run failed quietly because a generator had this Mac's home folder written into it. The step reported success anyway. That's why the warning exists now.
