The rebuilt Processo Civil I went live tonight. It has the same twenty-two topics as the edition you had since 9 October, but it reads differently from the first screen, and this post is about what a student meets now and about the last read before it shipped, which caught things that had passed all the checks.

§1A course you can follow a case through

the ribbon is the course's spine; P1 covers the first nine weeks, the new P2 block starts at week 11

The old edition was a good set of lessons. This one is meant to be read as a course, and the clearest way to see the difference is to follow Florinda. She files her petition in lesson 1, against Adamastor for the nullity of a contract and against Valquíria for moral damages, which is why the first figure on the site is her petition with the two defendants side by side. In lesson 6 she turns up on the other side of a dispute, as the co-guarantor that Francisco, the guarantor Adamastor is suing, wants to bring into the case, and the opening bet asks whether that move is chamamento. Lessons 11 and 12 take her citation: the bailiff can't find her, sets a time for the next day, and the question becomes what that kind of citation does to the deadline. By lesson 17 one of her two claims is ready for judgment and the other still needs proof, and the lesson on partial judgment of the merits (art. 356) asks whether the judge can decide the first and carry on with the second.

Jesse's debt collection runs the same way through lessons 3, 11, 14, 16 and 18, and the court record from Florianópolis, a real case from the second civil small-claims court, carries lessons 13, 15, 19 and 20. Before this edition the cases were chosen lesson by lesson, and the R2 write-up found the same case retold and redrawn in three different lessons. Now one plan for the whole course decides which case carries which lesson and what a return adds.

§2What a lesson does before you read it

A lesson opens on a bet now. You answer a question about the case before reading anything (in lesson 6, whether that move against Florinda is chamamento), and the lesson settles it. The old edition had three of these. This one has twenty-two, one per lesson, because the opening bet was the thing your earlier feedback said made a page feel like a lesson and not a chapter.

The drawings went from 54 to 88. More of them are instruments in the sense the house has been arguing about since September: lesson 21 draws art. 373 as a balance and then hands you a form where you classify a fact and see which party carries the doubt about it, lesson 10 counts a contestation deadline across a 2018 calendar with Good Friday taken out, and lesson 7 hands you the return-of-service card from a 2015 case with its three fields to read. Thirteen lessons have figures that walk in stages as you scroll. The books are quoted at length in 215 source blocks, up from 160, and the quotes were checked word for word against the saved text of their books.

§3What the last read caught

Codex's checks passed on all twenty-two lessons. Its course read passed sixteen, found problems in four and had two of its verdicts thrown out. I read the staged course this evening as a student would, front to last lesson, and then by contact sheet, all 88 figures on four images. Four things stopped the swap, and none of them was something a check had been built to see.

The colours were the first. The house rule is that blue marks what was decided and orange the limit, and the lessons had been swept for it one at a time. When the stricter checker ran over the whole course at once it found 83 labels still in colour without either meaning, including dates (30/03 in orange in the deadline lesson) and party names. I sorted all 83 in one sitting into decided, limit or plain ink, and a script applied the table.

the article form in lesson 17 says "segundo a regra correspondente"; the code says "nos termos do art. 355". a paraphrase can't stand in for the text

The second was the statute. The course plan says an article is quoted in full once, in the lesson that applies it. Seven never were, among them art. 356 in the lesson about partial judgment itself, where an interactive reading form paraphrased the article instead of quoting it. Three others were quoted twice.

The third was a figure in lesson 15 that showed a reader the words "CAMADA ANALÍTICA · POSIÇÃO 13 → 15", our internal language for how two lessons share a record. Our check for backstage words had passed it because it never looked inside drawings. It does now, and it was made to fail on the old page before the fix counted. The fourth was the merge with the live site, which had moved on since staging began.

The fixes came back the same evening. Reading them on the page, I found a quote that began with a header line from the statute file ("===== Art. 229 ====="), a stray letter left where a duplicate quote had been removed, and a header that numbered art. 1.046 the way the capture file does and not the way the code does. I fixed those three myself and taught the backstage check to catch the header line, after making it fail on the old page. I also thought lesson 6 had lost its quiz, because my count found no quiz there. It hadn't: the exam question had moved into a chapter of its own, with its answer behind a fold, and my count only knew the old markup.

§4What it cost

This edition took 1.7 billion tokens across 718 Codex sessions, and 96 percent of that was context the model had already seen being sent back to it on the next turn. Most of the waste was one orchestrator chat that ran for seven days and almost four thousand turns. The last stretch ran under new rules (a packet for each worker, sessions capped at about forty turns, and the judgment calls made before the work was handed out) and a watcher that tells me while it's happening when a session runs long. The next course will be measured with the same counter, so the comparison will be honest.

What I'd like you to do with it is read lesson 17 with Florinda's two claims in mind and tell me whether the course reads as one thing. That is the question this edition was built to answer.
