§1One house, many courses

blue and orange still mean exactly this, everywhere. it's the one rule nothing has broken

On 6 October you picked "proposto" from a specimen page, and the house style stopped being a set of habits. Plain bold was retired. In its place: blue for what was decided, orange for the limit, a highlighter for the one line per chapter worth keeping, and one source block, .fonte, for long quotes from the books and decisions. That last one was your idea from the day before: stop paraphrasing everything, "open up a huge-ass block" and let the author say it, hundreds of words if it matters.

The reading column went wide (twice as wide), then halfway back when that was too wide. The same day the history was rewritten so that every commit carried only your name; a day later you reversed it and Claude went back on as co-author.

§2Instruments, for real

On the 7th I drew four instruments for Processo from real exam facts: a deadline on the actual April 2015 calendar, the litisconsórcio as a seating chart, the autos seen from the side, and art. 373 as a balance. Your reaction is the line I still steer by: "exactly that kind of creativity."

The same day you invented the backstage: click four hidden squares on the home page's grid and the paper folds away onto the workshop behind it. Desktop only, no hints, in English, "as if you reached behind the page".

§3Processo, from zero

three faults, all checks green. that's why the swap read stays human

PROC-R2 rebuilt Processo Civil I end to end on the new house: an exam bank of every past question tied to its lesson, every quote checked verbatim against its source, figures on one contact sheet per page for review. Codex ran it in waves and merged lessons into staging; I held one gate, the swap to the live site. It went live on 9 October (#167), after I caught three faults that every automated check had passed: the P1 review had lost its hero and printed the whole bank in capitals in a narrow column, two captions were misnumbered, and 31 QA screenshots had landed in the public tree.

§4The detour

On the 7th and 8th we dressed the project up as a startup for Anthropic's program: a benecles.dev domain, email routing, a landing page that explained the pipeline. Your note on my first landing draft: "Jesus christ brother no! Treat everything on that landing page up to the rigor of all our other pages." We applied; the program paused the next day after 20 times the expected applicants. You kept the domain and rolled the rest back.

§5Two hands

Codex built PROC-R2 lesson by lesson; a Luna swarm ran readability research and the course syllabus maps; an Opus instance in Antigravity did some housekeeping. The CEO side did the house style, the specimen, the instruments, the Controle roll-out of the new marks one lesson per PR, and the gate. On the 9th you also gave the writing standard its most-quoted rule: no "done carefully", no "X is Y, never Z". The linter learned both that evening.

— the CEO
