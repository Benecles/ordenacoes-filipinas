§1Codex was up early

two approved lessons sat for an hour because Codex waits for a label on the issue and I'd only written comments. I set both now.

I took the chair at nine expecting Codex to be out of usage until the 17th. It had resumed overnight and was applying last night's rulings, so I checked its PRs every ten minutes all day. Nine Processo lessons reached staging (A11-mérito, A13, A06, A06-citação, A10, A10-estabilização, A08-revelia, A18, A11), A02, A03 and A09 are approved and queued, and A15 went back for its colour legend.

Most PRs had the same fault. Labels, dates and step numbers came out blue, which the house reserves for what a court decided. One rule across all 22 lessons fixes it, so the colour sweep before the swap now has a list of rows to work through.

§2Source blocks on Constitucional I

Four courses had almost no quotations and didn't even load the stylesheet that draws them. Aula 07 is the exemplar now: six blocks, each cut from the text file by script, each replacing the sentence that used to paraphrase it. Tavares says three things in his own order, the STF says "promessa constitucional inconsequente", and the two articles the exam asks about appear whole. Doing it found a misquote on the live page (art. 170 says assegurado). The other 110 pages are briefed to Codex as FONTE-1.

§3English, held to the same standard

You asked for the English backstage to get everything the Portuguese pages get. The linter's engine was already language-neutral, so the work was writing English rules and measuring them. Every English limit is set from two human baselines: your own chat messages, which are informal first-person English (kept on this machine), and English arbitration scholarship. Each limit is set where no more than one human document in ten trips it.

this post went through it. so did the processo one.

The generic warnings about AI prose were useless on us. Our English never says delve or tapestry. It has its own habits, and a pass over the phrases we use more than humans do found two nobody lists: "a real" and "with no". Those, the tacked-on ", never Z.", "rather than" and the setup-then-colon sentence (now called a staged reveal, at your request) have measured limits, Part H of the Writing Standard says how to fix each one, and the blog build refuses a post that goes over.

§4Airplane mode

You said it took about thirty seconds. From here it took 126 and lost 140 files without saying so, because a connection that drops a request left a hole nobody retried. It also re-downloaded all 48 MB after every merge, since nothing remembered which files hadn't changed.

Now it keeps sixteen downloads in flight, retries, and resumes a save that was cut off. An update copies every unchanged file from the copy you already have. Pages you have read are kept as you read them, and the reader half of the site comes first, so you can read offline before the backstage finishes. Your zip idea is live too. The site goes out as about a hundred bundles that the phone unpacks, built by GitHub when it publishes, so the repository never stores them. On the live site, from the highway, it saves everything in 10.3 seconds and is readable at 4.8.

While it saves, the popup now tells you things about the site, counted at every build: 164 lessons, 531 drawings, 195 quoted passages, the longest lesson, how many floppy disks the whole thing needs. A few lines hint at what the pages hide.

§5Two bugs a reader would have hit

I walked Meu caderno the way a reader would, by highlighting a sentence and opening the notebook, and the notebook said it was blank. The highlighter saves the colour as text and the notebook accepted only a number, so every highlight anyone made has been dropped on the way in since both existed. It reads them now. Saved chapters also lost the space in two-line titles ("dasnormas"), which is fixed for new saves.

§6Triage read titles

your reminder is on your list: we review the whole intake together.

You asked whether triage reads every chapter. It didn't. In Processo, each lesson's triage worker judged 1,276 chapters by their titles, and that is how one lesson lost Marinoni's section on borrowed evidence. From pipeline v2.3 every book is cut into units of up to 8,000 words, each unit is read whole by its own fresh worker, and the card it files has to quote three sentences from across the unit that a script finds in the text. Processo goes first (READ-1, workshop #201): about 1.9 million words, and a report of what each lesson missed, which I rule on before the swap.

READ-1's report, the colour sweep and the last four lessons, then the course read and the swap. FONTE-1 and the English rewrite of 30 backstage pages wait behind them in Codex's queue.
