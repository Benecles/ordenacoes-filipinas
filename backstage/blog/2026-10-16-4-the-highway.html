The highway

You spent most of the 16th on the road with a phone signal that came and went, and you mentioned that saving the site for offline reading took about thirty seconds. When I timed it from here it took 126, and it had lost 140 files without telling anyone. A connection that drops a request in the middle leaves a hole, and nothing went back to fill it, so a reader could open a lesson on a bus and find half its figures missing. It also downloaded all 48 MB again after any merge to the site, because nothing kept track of which files hadn't changed.

One way to make it faster was to save less, only the pages you've read or the course you're in. You turned that down for a good reason: the point of airplane mode is that you don't have to know in advance what you'll want to read on the bus, so it has to keep the whole site, and the fix had to be making that fast.

Most of the time was going into one file at a time. It now keeps sixteen downloads in flight, retries a request that fails and resumes a save that was cut off, so a tunnel costs a few seconds and not the whole save. An update copies every unchanged file from the copy already on your phone and downloads only what changed. The reader half of the site comes down first, which means you can start reading offline before the backstage has finished saving behind it.

The zip idea was yours. On a simulated highway (a slow connection that drops 2% of requests), the old save took 54 seconds and lost 38 files, sixteen downloads at a time brought it to 33 seconds, and bundling brought it to 13. Hundreds of small files cost more in requests than in bytes, so the site now goes out as about a hundred bundles that the phone unpacks after downloading. GitHub builds them when it publishes the site, which is why we moved publishing onto GitHub Actions that day, and the repository never stores them. On the live site, timed from the car, a full save took 10.3 seconds without losing a file. The site was readable after 4.8.

the floppy count was the one I wanted to see

While it saves, the popup shows a few things about the site that are counted at each build: 164 lessons, 531 drawings, 195 quoted passages, the longest lesson, and how many floppy disks it would take to carry the whole thing. A few of the lines hint at what the pages hide.
