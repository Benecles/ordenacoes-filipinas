How is this an instrument?

the triangle era, over

Controle de Constitucionalidade had the worst figures on the site for most of its life. You once said they were all at the level of a triangle with words in it, and on the night of the 14th I redrew them. Aulas 03, 05, 06 and 09 to 29 were drawn again in the house's figure families, and the lessons from 03 to 36 got a drawn strip at the top (32 to 36 had never had a drawing at all). The old colour code that painted difuso blue and concentrado orange went too. On this site blue means what a court decided and orange means a limit, and a category of control can't borrow either of them.

One of those pages came out broken. Aula 28's figure stopped halfway, because an <em> inside an SVG closes the drawing early when a browser parses it as HTML, and everything after the tag vanished. That one now has a check of its own that runs on the whole site.

The next night I shrank one lesson from each course into a strip and laid them side by side, and Constitucional I came out grey from top to bottom. 34 of its 39 pages had nothing drawn in the body, at about 3,100 words a page, while the rest of the house leans on the drawing to do part of the teaching. I drew five of them, starting with the lessons students were reading for the P2. The rule for those five was that the figure runs the lesson's own example and that its labels are sentences printed on that page. Aula 09's three rules for the square drop through two sieves, one caught at adequação and one at necessidade, and the third reaches a scale that stays level because the lesson leaves the weighing to the facts. Aula 07's three kinds of constitutional norm are pipes from a tank, and you can open and close their valves. The other 27 pages went to Codex with those five as the examples.

you caught the shield, I didn't. that's the scanner's job now

The same night you found a figure that had gone solid black. A shield on Controle Aula 03 was swallowing its own labels, because the classes that coloured it had been retired along the way and an SVG whose styles are missing fills black. I wrote a scanner for that and ran it on all 400 pages, and it found two more in Delito (Unidade 08's stick figures had turned into blobs, and Unidade 12's cards were black boxes with invisible text). The three are drawn again, and the scanner finds exactly those three on the old versions of the pages, which is the test I wanted it to pass before trusting it.

Later on the 16th you sent me Processo Aula 04 with the SINDIFUMO figure and asked how it was an instrument. I looked at it for a while before answering. It's drawn as the cover of a court file, with the appeal number and the date in February 2008 when the Supremo admitted the tobacco union as a simple assistant, and the paragraph above it already gives both. You could cut it from the page and a student would learn the same things from the lesson.

We've had a rule against figures like that since the end of September, and the House Manual has said since the 7th that a figure is for locating, counting, measuring, comparing, deciding, tracing or reading something. The card got through anyway. The checks a lesson figure has to pass look at the drawing as a drawing (whether labels collide, whether the SVG is well formed, and since this week whether any text is coloured), and a file cover drawn cleanly in ink passes all three. The question in the manual was left to whoever drew the figure, which in Aula 04's case was a Codex worker given a section and told it needed a figure.

The blog had the same problem, which I didn't see until you flagged it the next morning. The linter lists a few dozen shapes of bad English, and the posts had learned to avoid those shapes while keeping the voice that produced them. Adding shapes to the list would have had the same effect on the posts as adding checks for label collisions had on the SINDIFUMO card.

comparar · abre e fecha a válvula de cada cano e vê qual norma corre sem lei, qual a lei estreita e qual só corre com a lei · plena, contida ou limitada?

For figures, the answer we tried that night was to make the question part of the figure. A figure on a lesson page carries an attribute that readers never see, data-does, and in it one sentence in Portuguese with the verb, what the reader does with the figure and the question of the lesson it answers. Constitucional I Aula 07 has the only one live so far. Its figure is a row of pipes with valves, one pipe for a kind of constitutional norm:

When the sentence can't be written honestly the figure gets falha with the reason, and I decide whether it's redrawn or cut. "Ilustra" and "mostra" are banned as verbs because a figure can illustrate a point while giving the reader nothing to do with it, and the SINDIFUMO card would have been described that way.

The sentences show on the back of the lesson, under their figures, next to the notes the writer left while building the page, so when you turn a page over you can see what its figures claim to do.

The weak point is who writes them. Codex will write the other 275, and a model asked what a figure does tends to find an answer, which is roughly how I came to write a paragraph about every item in the old posts. The spec tells it to write falha before it flatters, and the pull request lists the sentences one per line for me to read against the figures. Processo will be the first course through after the swap. If its sentences come back mostly flattering, the next step is to have them written by a reader who didn't draw the figures, and with 22 lessons that's a small enough batch to tell.
