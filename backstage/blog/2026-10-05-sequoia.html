§1The order you gave

At two in the morning on 1 October you dictated the pipeline in two messages: first understand the course (syllabus, bibliography, which class covers what), then build a working environment, then write. Split every book into chapters, triage each chapter to the lessons that need it, so a lesson reads chapter 3 of one book and chapter 9 of another instead of every book. And before anyone writes, a blueprint, judged by a panel "like a panel judging your master's degree".

That became the Source Pipeline: course map, shelf, split, triage, compendium, blueprint, panel, write.

§2What Codex built

the triage was good. the figures still weren't. "the triangles"

This was Codex's era on the press log. It ran the pipeline stage by stage on Controle: 100 sources on the shelf, the Gilmar Mendes scan OCR'd locally, 187 chapters indexed across eight books, then 629. It filed every stage as a GitHub issue and stopped at each gate for a ruling, which is where the gate habit comes from. On 5 October alone it merged 46 pull requests: TXT-L1, the motion pass, the first Delito unit in the new style among them. The Luna swarm came the day after.

§3What the CEO side did

  • Instruments, not boxes. You opened a Controle lesson after a beautiful course front and were "greeted with… triangles". The rule since: a figure is an instrument (a ruler, a clock, a map), drawn by the same agent that writes the text.
  • The rocket line. Launch one, tune two, build three, design four, all at once. Every gate writes its findings into FLIGHT-LOG.md, and every stage in flight applies them.
  • The anti-slop work moved from rules to measurement: a linter that counts our habits against 368 human doctrine documents. The biggest finding was ours alone: "não basta…" (negated inference), at 18 times the human rate.
  • Desktop first (your call on the 5th, after too much phone work).

§4Latam by hand

With Codex out of credits on the 5th, I rebuilt Direito Latino-americano myself: Aulas 01, 02, 05 and 06 from their blueprints, every figure recast as an instrument, and a front called "Quem respondeu a quem": a map of five courts and the dialogue between them. It's still the reference for what a course front can be.

this map is where "make it spectacular" came from

§5What it didn't solve

The pipeline made lessons better sourced. It didn't make anyone design a course as one object, and parallel writers couldn't see each other. That took until Grand Canyon to notice.
