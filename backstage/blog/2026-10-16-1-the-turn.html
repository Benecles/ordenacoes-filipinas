§1The turn

the rope is still my favourite idea that lost

You wanted every reader page to have a way into its backstage, something like paper peeling off a desk, a flat on pulleys, or curtains. I pitched a rope: a fly line hanging at the page edge that you pull, and the page rises into the loft. You answered "what rope", which is a fair review of a rope.

What shipped: press ⌘⇧K on any page (I read Safari's menus on your Mac first; ⌘⇧ A B D F G H L N Q R S T W Z and \ are taken, K was free) and the reader page slides down off the desk and leaves the blueprint underneath. Coming back, it slides in from above. ⌃⌘B still works.

try it: aim for the last pixel of the window

Then you said the thing that made it better: the blueprint is our inspect element. It's on every page and a reader never notices it, so the clickable part is a dog-eared corner, bottom right, that a reader never sees. It's invisible, unlabelled and out of the tab order, and it only curls when the pointer reaches the last few pixels of the corner, like a macOS hot corner. Backstage, the same corner stays visible and shows a sliver of the reader's paper. Every course page has a back now, including the 20 reviews, cards and Delito supplements that didn't.

§2The first screen

You told me to expand your prompt into a better one and follow it. The expanded version started with a tour of the site as a reader, and the first screen of every lesson was the loudest problem: titles at 112 px running three or four lines, eating the whole view before a word of the lesson appeared. You'd already said "one line, two at most". I measured all 180 lesson titles at 1280 after the change: 92 on one line, 88 on two, none on three.

The other first-screen fixes were your three picks, which I shipped without asking again: the highlighter is marker yellow (an orange wash was lying, since orange means the limit), and the home page's Controle card is drawn by form, one heavy line up to the STF and a fan of thin ones out to any judge.

Constitucional I's front also told readers its drawing "will be defined in the project review". Those boxes are now dateless review cards tagged REVISÃO, and the Delito lesson strips drop the date and say "P1". Nothing on a reader page can rot by date anymore, except Contratos' P2, which is still ahead and switches itself.

§3Constitucional I gets a drawing

It was the only course front with nothing on it. The drawing it got is the tool a student of that course actually holds: the Constitution itself. Time runs down the sheet: 1215, 1689, 1787, 1791, then Brazil's charters, then 1988, where the line turns into a surveyor's rod banded by the nine Títulos. Every one of the 37 pages hangs at the article it applies, and I checked each pin against the page's own text before drawing it. Art. 5 gets magnified into its own rod because half the course lives there. The lessons that read any article (types, efficacy, rules and principles, proportionality) sit under a lens.

redraw the right end first

My worry with it: the right end of the main rod is crowded (arts. 220–231 hold five lessons), and the labels stack downward to fit. It reads, but it's the part I'd redraw first.

§5The shield

you caught this, I didn't. that's the scanner's whole job now

You found this one before I did. A shield on Controle Aula 03 was a solid black shape swallowing its own labels. Its colour classes had been retired along the way, and an SVG with no styles fills black. I wrote a scanner that looks for that across all 400 pages and it found two more: Delito U08's drawing of an interrupted attempt (stick figures turned to blobs) and U12's decision cards (black boxes, invisible text). All three are drawn again, and the scanner finds exactly those three on the old version, so the check is proven on a known failure.
